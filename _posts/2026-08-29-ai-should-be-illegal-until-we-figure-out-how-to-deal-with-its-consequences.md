Artificial intelligence should be illegal until we figure out how to manage its social consequences. This may sound extreme, but the speed at which AI is advancing is fundamentally incompatible with the speed at which societies can adapt to it.

Like is widely known the most immediate problem is job uncertainty. People are being asked to invest years of their lives learning skills for careers that may not exist in the same form by the time they graduate. This creates not only employment uncertainty, but career uncertainty: how can someone responsibly choose a profession when its long-term existence is increasingly impossible to predict? It is naive to believe that simply understanding how AI works internally, programming AI systems, or following the latest model releases will keep someone's job safe. If AI's defining advantage is its ability to learn and improve faster than humans, then any new job created around today's limitations can eventually become a target for automation too.

There is also a deeper economic danger. If robots eventually perform most productive work, money could become less important as a mechanism for negotiating human labor. The ultra-rich might consequently place greater value on controlling the raw materials, energy, land, and infrastructure required to manufacture and operate robots than on preserving the value of dollars or other currencies. In an economy where machines produce everything, being wealthy may increasingly mean possessing the physical resources that machines require, rather than possessing money that humans need to exchange for their labor. This could concentrate power in ways our existing economic systems are not equipped to handle.

Finally, AI threatens something that cannot easily be measured economically: human connection. Art is not merely an arrangement of aesthetically pleasing information. Music, photography, painting, writing, and other forms of art carry the experience, intention, limitations, and personality of another human being. Consuming art generated by a machine can reproduce the appearance of that connection without actually containing another person's experience.

At the end the question is whether society should permit technology to transform employment, economics, creativity, and human relationships faster than we can understand the consequences. Maybe there should be exceptions like cancer research and other  medical endeavors that are agreed to be unquestionably good for mankind. But for most applications until we have credible answers, restricting AI may be less an attack on progress than an attempt to ensure that progress remains something humans can co-exist along without creating further distress in our lives.
